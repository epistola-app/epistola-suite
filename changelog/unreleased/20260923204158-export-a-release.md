title: A catalog release can be exported exactly as it was released.
---

A release recorded a fingerprint and a promise. The content behind it lived only in the working copy,
so renaming a theme or deleting an image changed what an earlier release would give you — and
exporting always handed over the working copy anyway, labelled `-dev` once it had drifted.

Releasing now stores an immutable, deduplicated copy of every resource it contained, and the export
dialog offers the releases that kept theirs. Exporting one gives you that release: its resources as
they were, carrying the version, timestamp and fingerprint it was cut with, whatever has been edited
since. Releases cut before this cannot be rebuilt and are not offered.

Two things follow. Storage grows with genuine change rather than with releases — re-releasing
unchanged content stores nothing. And the bytes a release needs are no longer reclaimed when the
image or font they belong to is deleted from the working copy; a tenant backup carries them too.
